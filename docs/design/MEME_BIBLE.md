# Foul Fowl Meme Bible & Design Notes

> Ducks.
> Pheasants for the win.
> Swans OP.

Voice for every string in this document: affectionate, dry, and in on the joke. The target of the joke is the trope, the tier list, or the swans. The player is never the butt. Real communities are thanked, never impersonated. No trademarked game or company name is a product name inside the game; the parenthetical nods are for the builders only.

Shared words, used the same way everywhere:

- **Beak** - close-range kinetic weapons.
- **Talon** - mid-range beams.
- **Horizon** - long-range missiles.
- **Grand Roost** - the galactic council.
- **Hyper-Preened** - the final tech tier.
- **Evening Standard** - the default match.
- **PNN** - Perch News Network, the ticker.
- **The Moulted** - ancient roosts that woke up angry.
- **The Antherons** - the late, tall, extra-dimensional herons.
- **Guardian of Orn** - the old fleet on the core nest.
- **The Big Quiet** - the endgame crisis, whichever face it wears.
- **The Perch Review** - the in-world review desk. Fair on purpose.

## 1. Roster

Eight empires. Homes are seeded: each homeworld fits the body, and two friendly worlds lie within two lanes. A penguin map that is all desert is a bug.

Swans are the broken race, and the patch is social, not a silent nerf. The galaxy bands against them. Pheasants are the race a proud veteran plays when they want the win to be theirs. Ducks will still invoice everyone.

### The Pale Supremacy - Swans

**Cob-Empress Cygnara the Unbothered.** A cob is a male swan. She kept the title. The other option sounded like a stationery firm.

Pitch: Better than you at the thing you were about to do, and already bored of the demonstration.

Archetype: the banned S-tier - a research empire that also wins fights, with the manners of a fallen people who have not fallen.

| | |
|---|---|
| Bonus | +25% research output |
| Bonus | +15% weapon damage, all bands |
| Drawback | **Notorious Perfection.** Every empire starts at -40 relations. Coalition (see Design Ideas) trips against Swans at 30% of scored power instead of 35%, and the mutual-relations bribe the others feel is +15 rather than +10 |
| Signature | **Swans OP.** Ship movement +1. Research costs -15%. Printed in gold, first on the card. Tooltip: "Known issue. Shipped anyway." |

Homeworld is a rich terran: +1 food and +1 mineral over a standard capital. The almanac calls the extra "of course."

AI - **The Unbothered.** Researches toward a victory clock, accepts tribute, rarely insults, declares war when the math is already unkind. Does not dogpile a weaker neighbor for sport. If the player is winning, Cygnara's tone shifts one degree, which is her version of panic.

- Greeting: "You may speak. A moment has been allotted."
- Threat: "We dislike doing this. We dislike doing it poorly more."
- Peace: "Stop. The border may remain where you placed it."
- War: "This is a correction."
- Defeat: "How novel. Write it down. No one will believe you."

Archetype: the gunner's empire - fantastic weapons, a sportman's honor code, a factory bill for all that plumage.

AI - **The Sportsman.** Declares formal wars, keeps treaties, fights the current leader rather than the weakest neighbor, and will accept a rematch the turn a peace expires. Never frames, never breaks a ceasefire.

## 3. Tech Tree Naming

Six fields. Eight rungs each. Rungs 1-2 are early, 3-5 are the midgame choices, 6-8 are the evening-enders.

**Choice rule.** At rungs 3, 5, and 7, each field offers two applications. You take one now. The other stays available later at +25% cost. Owls (Wide-Eyed) may run both at normal cost. Chickens (One-Note) are shown only one. Crows exist to steal the one you skipped.

Terminal keys, unsellable and unstealable: **Hyper-Preened Kinematics**, **Hyper-Preened Genesis**, **Hyper-Preened Mandate**, plus the victory building **Departure Roost** (requires the first two).

### Flightcraft

1. **Downrange Charts** - Scouts stop getting lost on the way back from the same star. *(Scout range up.)*
2. **Warm Current** - A polite ionic breeze. Fleets arrive less tired. *(Fleet speed +1.)*
3. **Tailwinds** - Sublight, with someone finally leaning on it. *(Choice: speed, or fuel economy.)*
4. **Migratory Math** - Range becomes a number you may exceed on purpose. *(Supply range up.)*
5. **The Folded Sky** - Space, pinched. The pinching is licensed. *(Choice: tactical initiative, or strategic speed.)*
6. **Roost Lanes** - Stable lanes between perched stars. The geese have opinions. *(Lane travel unlocked.)*

## 4. Events

Fifteen. Player-facing text is the two sentences. The effect is the rule.

### 1. The Tier List Leaks

PNN publishes the tier list, and it is one sentence long. Every other empire suddenly remembers it has neighbors.

If a Swan empire exists, all non-swan empires gain +15 relations with each other for 25 turns and a Coalition casus belli against the Swans. If none exist, the same package hits the score leader at +10 relations for 15 turns.

### 2. Pond-Scum Leviathan

A soft moon with opinions has drifted into a lane, hungry and badly defined. Your scouts report that it jiggles with purpose.

The system becomes impassable to military hulls. A scout may spend 3 turns tagging it: the tagger's next Plumage tech costs -20%, and the lane clears. Sending military ships through anyway destroys the smallest ship in that fleet. The leviathan then leaves, unoffended, because there is nobody to offend.

### 3. The Guardian of Orn

At the core, something old is still on its nest and finds your century thin. It offers one warning volley and the dignity of turning around.

Reveals the core and spawns the Guardian at 120% of the strongest fleet anyone currently fields. Destroying it grants Orn-Plating (a unique hull module, one empire's copy) and a research cache. Firing and then retreating applies Guardian-Marked for 10 turns: that empire's fleets fight at -10% damage. The Guardian remains for someone else.

### 4. Tall Shapes

Scopes catch silhouettes that refuse to resolve into birds, patient as a bill. The Flocknet notes are very calm, which is how you can tell the author is afraid.

Arms the Antheron clock: in 40 turns a crisis fleet arrives at a random border system of the score leader. Any empire may spend 15% of its current research stockpile to add 15 turns. This delay can be bought twice in the whole galaxy, not twice per empire.

### 5. The Henhouse War

Two Moulted roosts wake on opposite rims and remember they hate each other more than they hate you. Both envoys assume the answer is yes.

Spawns two ancient one-system NPC empires if needed. Siding with one loans you a Titan Perch for 30 turns and tithes 10% of your industry each turn of the loan. Refusing makes both treat your lanes as a battlefield. Snubbing the side you did not pick adds them to the war immediately.

### 6. Placid, Until

A colony with a gentle reputation finishes a warhead with excellent manners. The dedication speech thanks everyone, and then the local weather ends.

Fires only if someone has unlocked the Very Polite Warhead. The most peaceful AI stance on the map flips to **Placid, Until**: soft speech, no grudge required before it uses a planet-killer. If the player owns the unlock, they choose Arm (-20 relations with everyone, warhead kept) or Mothball (warhead scrapped, +10 relations, credit toward Reviewed Well).

### 7. The Midgame Sit

Nothing is wrong, which is the problem. The queues are full of adequate choices, and PNN has started playing soft music.

Fires on the first turn after 70 where nobody is at war and no victory clock is inside 40 turns. The player must Stand Up: open a formal rivalry with a named empire (+10% industry for 10 turns, -10 relations with them, no war yet) or pull the Grand Roost 30 turns earlier. Geese and Pheasants, if AI and affected, always pick the rivalry.

### 8. While You Were Away

Your governors ran a full shift, which is what the toggle asked them to do. They have filed the digest in plain language, and they would like it noted that they did not sell the future.

The first time automation has acted for 10 consecutive turns, open the digest and allow one free revert of a single colony's queue to its pre-digest state. Once per game.

### 9. Letter of Marque

A damp freelancer offers to hurt your rival for a fee and a flag. The hull they showed you has been repaired more often than it has been built.

Pay 150 credits to spawn a privateer fleet that raids one chosen empire's trade for 12 turns. The victim learns it was you: -25 relations with them, +5 with their enemies. Declining costs nothing and gives PNN a one-line joke.

### 10. The Corner Colonist

An empire nobody was watching has parked colonies on the rim and a mine on a rock you filtered out. The scoreboard clears its throat.

The lowest-score AI that owns a rim world gains two free developments there and a scout. If the player owns the only rim worlds, the player gains one free development instead, and PNN praises the long way without the scare.

### 11. Snowball, Named

The leader's advantage has become self-aware, and children have drawn it as a circle with a beak. Even the penguins are calling it a snowball, and they would know.

The score leader gains +10% growth and +10% credits for 15 turns. The modifier's UI name is Snowball. Every other empire receives a one-turn bulletin of the leader's exact fleet and colony totals.

### 12. Perch Review

The Review Desk files its piece on your last twenty turns, affectionate and specific. Stars are attached, which is the part your admirals will pretend not to read.

Five stars if, in that window, you won a battle at a casualty exchange of 2:1 or better, or you advanced a victory clock: +10 morale for 20 turns, plus achievement credit. Two stars if you expanded and finished neither a development nor a tech: -10 morale for 10 turns, and the digest highlights one idle colony. Anything else is three stars and no modifier. The prose stays polite when the score does not.

### 13. Pure Missile Honesty

Your designers submit a hull that is missiles in a coat, and they look proud. Somewhere a Talon clears its throat like an old friend you stopped calling.

For 20 turns the player may build ships with an extra Horizon slot that cannot also mount Talons. Refitting back costs one turn in dock. One AI empire takes the same deal: Crows first, otherwise the lowest-score empire. Pheasants decline if they are the only candidate.

### 14. The Spreadsheet You Wanted

A Flocknet clerk slides over a workbook that describes one missile across forty tabs. It is beautiful, and it is also how evenings disappear.

Adopt it, and one lab locks for 6 turns, then Thesis Range is gained empire-wide. Shred it, and production rises +5% for 8 turns. The clerk is relieved either way. She has seen this workbook before.

### 15. Called to the Grand Roost

The minutes are ready before the galaxy is, and the envoys have counted heads, grudges, and moons owed. You may send a speech, a bribe, or a battleship-shaped silence.

Pulls the next council to occur in 8 turns, ignoring the turn-120 floor, if at least four empires have met. For this vote only, bribe and threaten are available even without Council Minutes: a bribe costs 20% of treasury and buys one ballot; a threat requires a fleet in the target's system, buys the ballot, and leaves relations at Uneasy afterward.

## 5. Design Ideas

Mechanics only. All ten are in scope for a single-threaded browser game if the colony stays the atom of the simulation.

1. **Colony grain, not citizens.** A colony is a population number, a world type, a queue of at most four items, a focus (grow, build, or research), and a governor toggle. No pop agents, no ethics, no tile workers.
   Rationale: That grain fits a two-hour game and 60 fps in a browser, and it keeps every economic decision on one readable screen.

2. **Evening Standard, with the doors printed.** Thirty-two systems, six empires, Swans seated unless the toggle is off, horizon at turn 200. The Grand Roost can be called from turn 120. The crisis clock arms at turn 170 if nobody has a victory inside 25 turns. The score screen lists, per empire, the nearest victory and an estimated turn.
   Rationale: A visible door does more for the midgame than any amount of encouragement.

3. **Three range bands and an autopsy.** Beak, Talon, and Horizon, as defined above. A battle is one system, aimed at 60-90 seconds, with a hard cap on drawn projectiles; overflow becomes a volley tick, not a particle. The autopsy then names the band that decided it, the retreat if there was one, and the single hull that overperformed.
   Rationale: A ship design matters only after the player can see why the last fight turned.

4. **Retreat with a receipt.** Any fleet may leave on its own turn. The slowest ship, or the most damaged on a tie, is lost or captured. No battle is a cage.
   Rationale: A priced retreat creates maneuver; a cage creates one blob.

5. **Fleet cap.** Base cap is 6. Geese are 9. A Titan Perch counts as 3. Overflow does not join the fight; it arrives next turn. The DOOMSTACK rename still fires at 8, so the joke survives the diet.
   Rationale: Several real fights per war will beat one arithmetic collision at the border.

6. **Automation that accounts for itself.** Repeat queues, auto-explore, and colony focus are the whole system. Every 10 turns the digest lists what changed, with one free revert. Automation cannot trade a tech, cancel a victory project, or declare a war.
   Rationale: Governors are a feature only if the player can catch them.

7. **Trade with a memory.** Techs may be gifted, sold, or stolen. The three Hyper-Preened keys and Departure Roost cannot move. Every successful transfer raises the seller's threat and is remembered as a favor or a grudge with a number on it.
   Rationale: The trade screen is half the genre, and a memory stops it from quietly handing over the win.

8. **Coalition, in the open.** At 35% of scored power, everyone else gains a Coalition casus belli and +10 relations with each other until the leader falls under 30%. Swans trip it at 30%, and the relations bonus is +15. The modifier is labeled Tier List when the target is Swans, and Snowball otherwise.
   Rationale: The honest patch for an overpowered bird is the galaxy's behavior, shown on the relations screen.

9. **Cheats with their names on them.** Nestling plays the rules. Flighted gives the AI +10% industry. Honk Admiral gives +20% industry, +20% research, and vision of the player's ship designs. Lights-Off Ledger adds full vision of the player's colonies. All four cards print their own modifiers before the game starts.
   Rationale: A named cheat is a difficulty setting. An unnamed one becomes a bug report.

10. **Bills, not slot machines.** Anomalies, the Guardian, marques, and the Perch Review each show the cost and the prize before the player commits. The event table itself is readable in an almanac screen.
    Rationale: Veterans will pay a hard bill, and they will quit a game that feels like it rolled them.

## 6. Victory, Defeat, and Loading Tips

Three victories, plus a horizon. All of them can fire against a living Swan empire, and the banner should know.

**Conquest.** Every other capital is yours or ash.

> The lanes are quiet, which is one way to hear a win. The Perch Review will call it decisive, and for once they will be using the small word.

If a Swan empire died in this game at your hand, add: `Against the swans.`

**The Grand Roost.** You hold Hyper-Preened Mandate, and the vote names you Supreme Bird. Population is the ballot. Bribes and threats work once each per empire per vote, then they are remembered.

> All in favor. The abstentions have been felt, and filed, and they do not count.

**Exodus.** Hyper-Preened Kinematics, Hyper-Preened Genesis, and a finished Departure Roost. You leave. The scoreboard stays, because of course it does.

> They left the galaxy in excellent order. The scoreboard remained behind, which is traditional.

**Called Game.** Turn 200, and nobody has taken a door. Highest score wins.

> Time, bird. You may press the other button. You may also be done.

**Banner variants**, one extra line, not a replacement:

- Player is Swans: `As expected.`
- Player is Pheasants: `Pheasants for the win.`
- Player is Ducks: `Ducks.`
- Player defeated a living Swan empire's chance, any victory type: `Against the swans. Tell your hatchlings.`

### Defeat

**Capitals gone.**

> The nest is ash and the lanes are someone else's. There is a rematch in the same galaxy seed, if the evening still has room.

**Someone else is Supreme Bird, and you refused vassalage.**

> The minutes list you as present. History will list you as nearby.

**The Big Quiet takes the homeworld.**

> The door opened on your side. The almanac will be very fair about this, which will not help.

**Called Game, and you are not first.**

> The horizon arrived before the comeback. The comeback has been noted in the margin.

**Defeat while playing the Swans.**

> Historic upset. The galaxy has framed the scoreboard. Scholars are unhappy. Good.

**Defeat while playing the Pheasants.**

> A fair loss. We hate it. The rematch button is the sporting one.
