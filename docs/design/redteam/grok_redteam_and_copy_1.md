## Part A - Red team (fun and authenticity)

Reviewed as a regular who has played the Orion games to death and will say so in the Discord if the evening is a caption on top of a spreadsheet. The merge is a real MoO2-lineage game: free movement, fuel range, jobs, a choose-one tree, three weapon bands, a council, printed AI cheats. The risks below are where that recognition wears off in the chair.

### 1. High - The starter kit already contains the winning band

The reference designs are the tell. A medium Horizon boat does about 43 / 33 expected damage at range 3 against shield 0 / 2, for four salvos. A medium Talon destroyer does about 25 / 14. Early battles are specified to end in 3-5 rounds, which is inside the missile window. Everyone starts knowing both the beam and the nuclear missile, and point defense is a Weapons tier-2 *option* sitting next to the missile upgrade, so the counter and the better missile are mutually exclusive for that era.

Pheasants are the veteran's race and their gun is the band that loses the opening. The triangle the designer is proud of shows up only after someone has spent a lab on the answer.

**Fix:** Starting knowledge is Talons, the drive, and the armours. The first Horizon part is a tier-1 option, beside Missile Base, so missiles are a lab choice. Tune Hatch Dart so four starting Talons beat four starting missiles at range 3 with no shields. Put Swat Mount on the tier-2 *core* (everyone who reaches the missile era receives the answer) and leave the tier-2 option as the missile upgrade. The choice becomes "greed the upgrade" versus "I already have the swat and I might skip the upgrade," which is a real dockyard argument.

### 2. Blocker - Several "choices" resolve to a sentence

These are selectable in the MVP tree and marked STRETCH in their effect: Sabotage Network, Stasis Field, Artificial Planet, Roost Lanes / Star Gate, Nest Bay (the fighter alt). Climate Tailoring and Gaia Transformation at least name a lesser MVP effect. A button that does nothing is how this audience decides the tree is unfinished, and it burns a Creative empire's whole fantasy on empty boxes.

**Fix:** Every option that can be clicked in the MVP has an MVP effect on the same row, written on the button. Park the stretch fantasy in the flavour of that effect. Suggested stand-ins, all reversible when the real system arrives: Sabotage Network  +15 spy score; Stasis Field  enemy combat speed -1 for rounds 1-2; Artificial Planet  one chosen Bare world becomes Desert and gains a size unit; Star Gate  +1 map speed between two colonies you name, until the gate building exists; Nest Bay  the carrier gets +1 combat speed and a Swat Mount's worth of point defense. Dead buttons leave the tree.

### 3. High - The fork is a coupon, and half the clicks are corridors

An unchosen option can be researched later at 150%, traded, stolen, or Cached. By the time the era matters, a careful player has both. Wide-Eyed and Cache are billed as the identities that own the other branch. They own a discount and a head start.

Beside that, Flocknet's spine is five battle computers that are the same part with a larger number, and you must click each one to reach the options that are actually decisions. That is the authentic ladder, and it is also the stretch of the evening where the player hits Next without reading.

**Fix, two parts.** An unchosen option cannot be *started* until two further tiers in that field are done. Trade, steal, and Cache still bypass the wait, so diplomacy and Crows stay the shortcut, and Owls remain the only empire holding both during the era the choice was about. And the core part of a tier is granted when the tier completes; the only click is the option. The designer still shows Pecking Logs becoming Grudge Ledger. You lose about a third of the research prompts and none of the parts.

### 4. High - The line order is decided by tonnage

Eight slots and a reserve that fills deaths is the right replacement for "overflow arrives next turn," which would feel like a bug. Largest-hull-first throws it away. A Titan sits on two seats. The Swat escorts and the Beak cruiser you built as the answer wait in reserve until the Titan has already eaten the first missile wave. Fleet composition matters only as "who is heaviest."

**Fix:** The fleet panel has a line order. Default remains largest first, so the AI and the lazy player still have a legal fleet. The player can drag. AI sets the order by role: point defense and the doctrine band in the line, troop ships and scouts in reserve. Reserves still fill holes as ships die.

### 5. High - Fights are a standing order plus a replay

Resolving the battle before the viewer, and making skip identical, is the right fairness rule. Keep it. Doctrine, bands, and the autopsy are real. They are still a spectator sport, and this audience will say the game plays its own fights. The range track existing only in a formula makes the three bands a designer tooltip.

Emergency Warp, as written, retreats on round 1 with no receipt. That is the correct Flightcraft pick, and it deletes the best new rule in the merge.

**Fix:** When the turn summary shows a battle, one command is issued before resolution, by the player and by the AI from doctrine: the stance you already have, plus a target priority (prefer a band, or "swats first"). The replay stays a replay. Paint the range strip on the battle view with Beak / Talon / Horizon written on it. Draw the first missile wave as darts being swatted. The volley tick is for overflow past the cap, never for that first wave. Instant Regret still leaves on round 1, and the receipt still fires. You regret faster. You still leave someone.

### 6. High - Swans are funny on the card and optional on the map

The gold line, the 13 picks, "Known issue. Shipped anyway," and a printed social patch are the right joke. They will carry the marketing screenshot for as long as you want.

They will not carry a second evening unless the galaxy actually hunts. Notorious is -20, and the war check is relation *below* -20, so Swans are not in the war zone on turn 1. The Coalition adds a casus belli, a relations sweetener, and a war-ratio of x0.8. The Unbothered will not dogpile, the Librarian will not declare before turn 80, the Patient Rock will not leave the ice, and the Dealmaker declares only under -50 or when shot. A patch the polite personalities are allowed to ignore is a caption. The soak band of 35-50% is two different games: "a bit much" and "they win half."

**Fix:** While Tier List is up, every Coalition member with no treaty against the Swans scores that war as if its aggression were 8. The push lasts 25 turns or until the Swans drop under the line, so a Swan player can survive by winning and shrinking. Print it on the card under the gold line: "They get hunted. That is the patch." Narrow the soak target to 40%, five points either side. Do not take picks off the card.

### 7. High - Ducks can adjourn the evening with a ballot

Votes are population. Ducks grow the fastest, live on the most climates, and are Liked on Sight: a candidate they vote for gains a tenth of their votes, and a candidate votes for itself, so the bonus is a self-bonus. The Dealmaker buys pledges. The first session that can crown a Supreme Bird is turn 50, before most empires have the guns the roster is about.

**Fix:** The charismatic bonus applies to someone else's candidacy only. Cap votes acquired by pledge at 15% of the votes cast. The turn-50 session publishes the count and cannot crown anyone. The next session can end the game. Soak check before you tune further: if Grand Roost wins are above a third of games that contain Ducks and end before turn 120, the cap wants to be lower, not the personality.

### 8. High - Three of the eight are not playing the card

**Penguins.** Tolerant says farmers make at least 1 food anywhere. Piscivore says off-table climates are 0, and the ledger's intent is that hydroponics are the food on hot rocks. Both traits are on the same race and the precedence is unpublished. Separately, the 8-pick floor *does* do real work (Bare and Crackle at 3 per size, no dome tech). The ice fantasy survives only if the food floor loses to the fish table and the heat cap still applies after the pop floor. Write that in one rule the tooltip can show. Until then the race is a tolerant with a paragraph.

**Crows.** Omniscient shows every planet and every fleet from turn 1, so the spy empire skips Explore. Espionage is a per-rival slider that eventually steals a random tech. That is fiddly, and it is not a decision. **Fix:** Drop full-map vision off this race. Give them treaties, treasuries, and ship designs without a battle. One spy target, not eight sliders. When a steal fires, show three techs and let them pick. Cache stays the twelve-turn button.

**Chickens.** Uncreative deals one random option and locks the sibling out of research. A seed that withholds Swat Mount during the missile era is a loss the player did not choose. **Fix:** Show both, keep one, forever. Trade and steal still recover the other. The drawback is commitment. The dice were the frustrating part.

**Geese**, for the same question. They do play differently: territorial war, fat hulls, troops, ten line slots. V-Formation does not. It caps at +25% by the sixth shooter, which a custom race can buy, and the extra Goose slots add guns rather than percent. **Fix:** The percent applies only to ships of the plurality weapon band in the line. A mixed blob does not get the folk art. Goose auto-design already wants one band. The DOOMSTACK rename stays cosmetic and should stay.

**The other four.** Swans (speed, accuracy, labs, a hunt), Pheasants (Talons, first blood, a bill), Ducks (growth, money, the vote, bad aim), Owls (both options, peace research, thin yards) will feel different in the first forty turns once findings 1, 6, and 7 are in. Do not add more traits to them.

### 9. High - The Guardian pays in the top of the beam ladder

Fixed 3000 HP is correct. Veterans can plan, and the soak can see it. No rubber band.

The loot includes the tier-8 Talon. The first empire to win that fight stops caring about the last two beam tiers, and every later autopsy gets flatter. The Orion memory you want is a unique module and a scar, not a skipped ladder.

**Fix:** The Guardian still mounts Last Glares. The prize is Orn-Plating, two techs at "your highest tier + 1," and nothing that skips a band. Guardian-Marked stays.

### 10. Medium - Exploit is a preset, and that trade is almost right

Presets, empire-wide food, universal overflow, and a governor that balances farmers remove the 1993 chores: sliders, freighters, empty queues. Do not bring those back. What they also remove is the economic mistake. If Capital is already the right answer, the colony screen is a report that still offers Manual as a lifestyle.

Food surplus at 1 credit per food equals a pop unit's entire tax, so a growth race's farmers are a mint. That will show up in the soak as Ducks and Chickens printing money. It is a second snowball hiding in the chore removal.

**Fix:** On the tutorial seed, measure Frontier-then-Industry against a locked Breadbasket on a high-farm, poor-mineral world at turn 40. If the gap is under 10% of output, the preset is too good; worsen Frontier's building list on farm-heavy rocks and say so in the governor log ("left food on the table at {place}"). Drop surplus food to 1 credit per 2 food so breadbaskets feed people and taxes stay the mint.

### 11. Medium - The late game is a museum of legal ships

Obsolete warnings and "upgrade queued designs" do not touch the fleet that is flying. Refit is stretch. The late evening becomes a shipyard queue while old hulls fight the last war in last decade's plate. One evening can survive that. The second evening is the complaint.

**Fix:** Promote a dumb refit to MVP. At a system with a shipyard, pay the positive industry difference, the ship is absent for two turns, the design swaps. No component puzzle.

### 12. Medium - The default opponents can adjourn the war, and the score aims the Coalition at the library

Unbothered will not dogpile. Librarian will not declare before turn 80. Patient Rock stays on the ice. Dealmaker sends invoices. Geese and Pheasants are the friction in the roster. Evening Standard seats Swans and then whoever the picker has left. Three polite neighbours produces the Midgame Sit, which is the design noticing it is bored and handing the player a button.

Score counts techs at 2. Owls and tech-buyers trip Coalition and Snowball for reading, including turns when they have no fleet. Interrupting an owl is the intended war. Declaring them the military leader because the library is large is a fake one.

**Fix:** The Evening Standard card names its three AI seats: Swans, one early-war personality (Geese or Pheasants), one schemer (Crows or Ducks). Say so on the preset. Keep the Sit as the failsafe for custom lobbies. Split the numbers: Called Game keeps the full score; Coalition and Snowball use pop, colonies, and fleet value. Label both.

### 13. Low - Screenshots you already earned, and the ones that will not happen by accident

You already have the gold Swan card, "Honk.", the DOOMSTACK rename, the autopsy, the words Tier List and Snowball, the Guardian's warning, and PNN. Those will get posted if the UI actually prints the words.

Stage these. They are presentation of rules you have:

1. The battle range strip with the band names, and the first missile wave drawn as darts (finding 5).
2. Defy as a card that lists the wars by empire. "Three empires declare war" is not a screenshot. "Brenda, Vesper, and Cygnara are at war with you" is.
3. Capitulation as a two-line surrender card, not a silent transfer of colonies.
4. The Perch Review as one panel with stars, not a toast.
5. An audible honk on the Goose border taunt.
6. Map climate labels using Ice and Jungle, so a Penguin game is recognizable in a crop.

### 14. Keep

Free movement and fuel range. Lanes would be a different genre; the wormhole is the corridor. Outposts are the soft bit: a range bubble with nothing to kill is a paint fill. Give an outpost one defensive mount, or accept that the border is colonies only.

Pooled food. Printed difficulty cheats. Battles resolved before they are shown. One species' traits remaining on a captured colony. Deterministic orders. The Swan card staying over budget. Role-based auto-design, which is the band decision; the button is the arithmetic.

### Answers to GDD 19

1. Free movement is right. The battle line fixed the doomstack collision. It did not create chokepoints. See the outpost rider above.
2. Doctrine alone is not enough agency. One pre-battle priority is. No clicks inside the replay.
3. 150% is too generous. Delay the other branch two tiers. Trade and theft stay.
4. Funny for many evenings only if the hunt is mandatory while Tier List is up. Tune to 40% ñ 5. Keep 13 picks.
5. Pooled food deletes a chore. Keep it. Reprice surplus food (finding 10).
6. Presets are the right surface. Prove the manual gap on one seed or stop pretending Manual is a path.
7. Ducks are too strong at the ballot. Self-bonus off, pledge cap on, first session non-binding.
8. Capitulation, a council that can actually crown, and an Exodus that needs every field at tier 7 will end the slog. A Coalition that is only a label prolongs it (finding 6).
9. The math is enough for 200 turns. The lineup and the pre-battle priority are the missing decisions. A single HP pool is right for this evening.
10. Line of 8 with reserves beats a fleet cap of 6. The missing piece is who starts in the line.
11. Cut the dead STRETCH options first. Then stop making the core computer/armour step its own prompt.
12. At Flighted, a competent player beats this AI, and that is acceptable if the default roster contains one early war and Honk Admiral remains the honest harder card. The failure mode is a peaceful map, not a stupid tactician.
